Coworker,
as a service.

The paradigm behind Beckett, and only the parts of it that are actually shipped. Spoiler: it is not a chatbot with a job title.

Software kept almost helping.

SaaS gave you tools; you still did the work. Chatbots gave you answers; you still did the work. Copilots gave you suggestions, and you were still the one driving. Coworker as a Service is the honest next step: you hand over the outcome, not the keystrokes.

Delegation is the interface.

You don't operate Beckett. You ask it, in the channel you're already in, in the words you'd use with a colleague. It sizes the ask itself — a sentence back if that's all it deserves, or a run it staffs, builds, reviews and opens a pull request for.

You manage outcomes, the way you would with anyone else on the team. If that sounds like less control, notice it's the exact amount of control you have over every human coworker you already trust — and unlike them, this one leaves a diff you can read before anything merges.

What makes a coworker, not a tool.

accountability

A checklist it wrote from your ask, a fresh reviewer grinding the diff against that checklist, and a pull request at the end of it.

memory

A cross-conversation knowledge graph, not a context window. It knows your stack and your taste tomorrow.

presence

It lives where the team already talks. Not another tab you forget to open.

judgment

It casts each stage of a run separately, so cheap work stays cheap and a hard build gets the firepower it needs.

honesty

A mid-flight note that didn't reach a live worker comes back as buffered, never as done. Receipts over reassurance.

character

Its personality is one file it can rewrite live, in the channel, because you asked it to. No redeploy.

A service you host.

There is no meter on this. Beckett is MIT-licensed and self-hosted: the service in Coworker as a Service is what it does for you, not what you owe monthly. No tenant model, no per-seat billing, no hosted control plane — it runs on hardware you control, under credentials you minted, and its marginal cost is your own Claude subscription.

A coworker you host is the only kind that's really yours. Fork it, rename it, rewrite its persona and point it at your Discord — the whole thing is in one repo.

What it doesn't do.

A coworker you can't audit is a liability, so the limits are part of the pitch. It doesn't merge unreviewed: every run passes a fresh reviewer, and work that needs a human parks with the reason instead of guessing. It doesn't hold your keys — credentials stay on your box, and it refuses provider API keys from the environment on purpose.

And it doesn't pretend to be finished. Beckett is one opinionated coworker for one Discord server, not a general-purpose agent platform. Generality is a cost someone else can choose to pay by forking.

Go meet one.

Ours is in the Discord. It will probably have opinions about your repo.
